Time tracking with Google Calendar™ and Google Sheets™: bill every hour you work

If your work already lives in your calendar — client calls, site visits, delivery blocks — you have a complete timesheet you're probably not billing from. The trick is getting those events into a spreadsheet where formulas can total them.

Step 1 — Get events into a sheet

Import your calendar into Google Sheets™ with one column per field: title, start, end, and calendar. A sync tool does this in one click and can refresh hourly, so the timesheet stays current without exports.

Step 2 — Compute hours

With start and end as proper date-times, duration is a subtraction: =(C2-B2)*24 gives hours. Format as number, not time.

Step 3 — Total by client

If you put the client name in the event title ("Fairway — review call"), split it out with =SPLIT(A2, " — "), then total with =SUMIF(ClientCol, "Fairway", HoursCol). A pivot table gives you the whole month by client in seconds.

Step 4 — Keep it live

The difference between a report and a system is automation: an hourly auto-sync means the sheet is always up to date when the invoice run comes. Look for a tool that updates rows in place (no duplicates on re-sync) rather than appending fresh copies of everything.

Common pitfalls

  • All-day events — exclude them from hour totals or they'll add 24-hour blocks.
  • Duplicates — naive exports append every run; you want ID-matched updates.
  • Recurring events — make sure each occurrence lands as its own row.
