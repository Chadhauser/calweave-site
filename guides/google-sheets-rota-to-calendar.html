Build the rota in a spreadsheet. Let the calendar update itself.

Every small team's scheduling ends up the same way: the rota lives in a Google Sheet (because grids are how humans plan shifts), and then someone re-types it into a calendar so people actually get reminded. The re-typing is the bug. Here's the sheet-first method where the calendar follows the spreadsheet automatically.

Step 1 — structure the rota as one row per shift

Grid layouts (staff down the side, days across the top) are lovely to look at and useless as data. Keep a planning tab however you like, but maintain — or generate with formulas — a flat tab where each row is one shift:

  • Title — “Sarah — Front desk”
  • Start / End — full date-times (2026-09-08 09:00), which sidesteps every timezone ambiguity
  • Description / location — optional columns that flow onto the event

Step 2 — push rows to the calendar

Point a sheet-to-calendar sync at that flat tab and a shared “Rota” calendar: every row becomes an event; staff subscribe once and get shifts on their phones with normal Google Calendar™ reminders. No app to adopt, no logins to manage — everyone already has a calendar.

Step 3 — handle changes like an adult system

Rotas change constantly, and this is where DIY breaks: re-running a naive script duplicates every shift, and a wrongly-filtered “delete then recreate” approach has emptied more than one team's calendar. The two properties that make sheet-to-calendar safe are ID-matched updates (edit the row, the event updates — no duplicates) and a delete safety cap (a mangled sheet physically cannot mass-wipe the calendar). Insist on both from whatever tool or script you use.

Beyond rotas

The same one-row-one-event pattern covers content calendars, job bookings, deliveries, lesson schedules and maintenance visits — anywhere the plan is naturally a table and the reminder is naturally a calendar. And it runs in reverse for reporting: sync the worked calendar back to a sheet for hours and payroll.
